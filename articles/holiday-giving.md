Picking a tag or dropping off a toy is a simple way to do the season with kids. Most entries below are 2026 programs whose own pages post a place, a date, and, for volunteer shifts, a minimum age. A few neighborhood tag trees are included because recent local comments say they are up now. Those notes say to call ahead, and I do not invent hours. Tags and bins run out, so check before you go. Dated holiday events also land on the [Christmas](/christmas/) hub.

## Giving trees

[Redmond Town Center's Giving Tree](https://redmondtowncenter.com/events/973-giving-tree) is the most straightforward public tag tree I found. It runs November 10 through December 17. Pick a tag from the tree near the sleigh in Center Court, buy the new clothes or gift on the tag, and return it unwrapped to Guest Services at the Town Center Management Office, next to Kitanda and across from the Marriott, by December 17. Guest Services is open Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. The gifts support young people in the community, including through Friends of Youth.

[Friends of Youth's Giving Tree](https://www.friendsofyouth.org/givingtree2026) also runs November 10 through December 17. You can shop the organization's Amazon wishlist and drop the gifts at the Friends of Youth office at 13116 NE 132nd Street in Kirkland. The official guide lists drop-off hours as Tuesday through Thursday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Business partners host tags too. Start with the public page if you want to give without hunting for a tree.

[East Shore Unitarian Church](https://esuc.org/holiday-giving-tree-2/) in Bellevue has a Giving Tree in the church foyer from November 17 through December 13. The church calls it a good opportunity for family giving. Tags support Friends of Youth, Hopelink, The Sophia Way, Sound Health, and Youth Care, and are generally $25 to $50. Pick a tag, buy the gift or gift card, and return it with the tag attached by December 13. The church is at 12700 SE 32nd Street.

The [Snoqualmie Valley Kiwanis Giving Tree](https://kiwanisgivingtree.weebly.com/gift-purchasers1.html) places tags at more than 80 spots in the valley, including all of the valley libraries. North Bend, Snoqualmie, and Fall City libraries are on this year's host list, along with city halls, the Snoqualmie Fire Department, and many shops. Tags started going out the week of November 16. Each tag lists the age and gender of a child, not a name. For teens 14 to 18, many tags have a QR code for a gift-card donation. You can return the gift to any tree, not only the one where you picked the tag. The public page asks that gifts be back by December 11, when hosts start sending them on to the Christmas store. Later gifts may still be collected. If you cannot get to a tree, the same page lists a ship-to address at Edward Jones, 7329 Better Way SE, Suite A-105, in Snoqualmie. The [2026 host list](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/e/2PACX-1vR8K-vWyljLrtAnyB5_1Sm2DDbnQJB6-xTcuGtG9WqTtUkJd5mNhsUNgeq0Nb6AONB_pUmMXZsR3A74/pubhtml?gid=0&single=true) is on the Kiwanis site. North Bend City Hall is on that list.

[Holy Family Parish](https://www.hfkparish.com/group/outreach-and-social-justice-ministries) in Kirkland posts an Advent Giving Tree on its official ministries page. The parish says the trees are placed in the narthex on Thanksgiving Day. The church is at 7045 120th Avenue NE. A return deadline is not posted, so check at the church or call (425) 822-0295.

[Jubilee REACH](https://www.jubileereach.org/elves) in Bellevue is running Jubilee Elves for 2026, supporting Bellevue School District families and other local programs. You sponsor a specific child and put together a gift package, about $75, with a sweater or sweatshirt, socks, a $25 gift card, holiday candy, and an optional small gift. Packages are checked in at the Jubilee REACH Center, 14200 SE 13th Place, from November 30 through December 3, 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. If a church or community-group list is full, the [general signup](https://www.jubileereach.org/elvesgifts) is the place to start.

A few shop and church trees are harder to confirm on a website, but recent comments on Eastside and Kirkland threads say they are running this season. Commenters report a giving tree at [The Pizza Coop](https://www.thepizzacoop.com/) in Woodinville, at 13317 NE 175th Street. I could not find 2026 hours on the restaurant's site, so call ahead. A commenter also says [Snapdoodle Toys](https://snapdoodletoys.com/pages/visit) puts out a tree around Black Friday; the Kenmore shop is at 7330 NE Bothell Way, and there are also shops in Kirkland, Redmond, Bellevue, and Issaquah. The store did not post 2026 tag-tree details that I could find, so call or check their Instagram before you go. In Kirkland, a commenter says St. John Vianney, at 12600 84th Avenue NE, has a tree. I could not confirm 2026 details on the parish website, so call (425) 823-0787 before you go.

I did not find a confirmed 2026 public tag tree at Bellevue Square, Crossroads, or Totem Lake, or a posted Salvation Army Angel Tree tag map for Eastside malls. If you hear of one, [suggest it](/suggest/).

## Toy drives

[Les Schwab and KING 5](https://www.lesschwab.com/article/community-stories/2026-western-washington-toy-drive.html) are collecting new, unwrapped toys from November 2 through December 18 at Western Washington Les Schwab Tire Centers. Eastside stores include Bellevue at 12828 Bel Red Road; two in Redmond, at 2355 Bel Red Road and 6651 East Lake Sammamish Parkway; Kirkland at 12410 128th Lane NE; Bothell at 19401 Bothell-Everett Highway; Kenmore at 6330 NE Bothell Way; two in Renton; plus Maple Valley and North Bend. You can also shop the drive's Amazon registry, which ships to the Redmond store.

[EvergreenHealth](https://www.evergreenhealth.com/about-us/ways-to-give/toys-for-tots/) is partnering with the KIRO 7 Cares Toy Drive, which benefits The Salvation Army and Toys for Tots. The hospital is collecting new, unwrapped toys from November 9 through December 4 at clinics and hospital entrances in Kirkland, Bothell, Redmond, Kenmore, Woodinville, and Duvall, among others. Check the hospital's list for the exact desk or lobby at each site.

[KidVantage](https://kidvantagenw.org/event/winter-wishgiver-van-event/) (formerly Eastside Baby Corner) closed general donations at its hubs on November 14 for the rest of 2026. Families can still bring a new toy to the Winter WishGiver van at Kirkland City Hall, 123 Fifth Avenue, on Tuesday, December 8, from 10 a.m. to noon. Groups hosting a [Winter WishGiver collection drive](https://kidvantagenw.org/winter-wishgiver-collection-drives/) can drop those items through Friday, December 11.

[Issaquah Food & Clothing Bank](https://issaquahfoodbank.org/special-programs50/) is collecting $25 gift cards to Fred Meyer, Target, or Amazon for its [Holiday Gifts program](https://issaquahfoodbank.org/holiday-gift-program), which helps families with children 18 and under. You can drop off or mail cards at 179 1st Avenue SE in Issaquah, or donate funds through the end of the year and the food bank will buy the cards. Staff hours are Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m.

[Vision House](https://visionhouse.org/give-items-holidays) in Renton has a Christmas Amazon wishlist chosen by families it serves. Purchases ship to the organization's office and are given to parents in time for Christmas. The page does not post a 2026 cutoff, so check it before you order.

The [North Creek and Skyview PTSA Food Pantry](https://northcreekptsa.com/Article/View/December-Holiday-Gift-Card-Drive) is collecting $25 gift cards to Target or Fred Meyer for pantry families, with a goal of 150 cards. Drop a physical card in the locked box in the main office at Skyview Middle School, 21404 35th Avenue SE in Bothell, by December 11, or follow the [digital gift card instructions](https://skyviewmsptsa.org/Page/Pta/Gift%20Card%20Instructions). Donations are set to go to families on December 16.

Boys & Girls Clubs of Bellevue, [Bellevue LifeSpring](https://www.bellevuelifespring.org/holiday-giving/), and Rainier Athletes are running ['Tis the Season](https://bgcbellevue.org/tis-the-season-is-here/) for 2026. You can buy a requested toy or gift card through their Purposity list, and the gifts go to a holiday gift room at the Lake Hills Clubhouse, where families choose for their kids. You can also shop in person and drop gifts on December 4 or 5; email the organizers to schedule that drop-off. The gift room opens to families by appointment on December 9. I did not find a posted public tag tree in the club lobbies, which is how this program sometimes gets described.

## Family volunteering

Many regular food-bank shifts have a minimum age of 15 or 16, which rules out younger kids. A few 2026 options are more open.

[KidVantage's Issaquah Hub](https://kidvantagenw.org/location/issaquah-hub/) at 1510 NW Maple Street takes individual volunteers ages 12 and up. Ages 12 and 13 need a one-to-one adult with them. Sign up for a shift on the hub page. All locations are closed November 26 and 27 for Thanksgiving, and from December 21 through January 1.

[The Sophia Way](https://www.sophiaway.org/volunteer) has Donation Center assistant shifts in Bellevue on Wednesdays from noon to 2 p.m. Ages 16 and up can volunteer on their own. Ages 10 to 15 need a parent or guardian. The official page asks you to email volunteer@sophiaway.org for the address. This is a women's shelter program, so it is a sorting shift, not a place to drop off children's toys.

[Issaquah Food & Clothing Bank](https://issaquahfoodbank.org/volunteer) says most volunteer roles are for ages 15 and up, and each shift lists its own age rule. On Thanksgiving morning, November 26, the [Issaquah Turkey Trot](https://runsignup.com/Race/Volunteer/WA/Issaquah/IssaquahTurkeyTrot) has Gobble Wobble stations from 8:00 to 10:30 a.m. that the organizers describe as a good fit for families, youth, clubs, or groups. Sign up on the official volunteer page, and check that a shift is still open.

[Hopelink](https://www.hopelink.org/ways-to-help/volunteer/) welcomes volunteers ages 16 and up, and some roles require 18, after a background check and a virtual orientation. Group shifts, including at the Kirkland warehouse, also require everyone to be at least 16. October through December fills early.

Holy Family Parish in Kirkland also hosts a [Thanksgiving Community Dinner](https://www.hfkparish.com/group/outreach-and-social-justice-ministries), a restaurant-style meal on Thanksgiving Day for people who would otherwise be alone or go without. The parish says many volunteers are needed that day, and during prep the week before. Email hfkthanksgiving@gmail.com to ask about a shift. A minimum age is not posted, so ask before you bring younger kids.

## Tips for doing it with kids

Let a child pick the tag or the toy, then shop for that one request. Many programs ask for new, unwrapped gifts, and teen tags often ask for gift cards. If a volunteer page lists a minimum age, take it seriously. Showing up with a younger child can turn into a no. Check the page the day before you go, because trees lose tags and bins close when they are full. This guide is for the 2026 season. Next year's dates will be different.

[Meeting Santa](/articles/meeting-santa/) and [Christmas lights](/articles/christmas-lights/) are the other lists for this stretch of the calendar.
